High-Downforce 4-Element Rear Wing

A rear-wing concept developed after aero efficiency had improved but the vehicle still lacked the required downforce.

PROBLEM

Efficiency improved, but total downforce remained too low

After the drag and aero-efficiency problems had been addressed, the vehicle was still producing only about 200 N of downforce in the relevant rear-wing condition. The next objective was therefore to increase aerodynamic loading rather than continue optimizing drag alone.

DESIGN RESPONSE

Four elements to raise downforce from 200 N to 350 N

I developed a 4-element rear wing that increased downforce from approximately 200 N to 350 N, a gain of about 150 N. The concept solved the immediate downforce problem, but the improvement came with two important tradeoffs: additional aerodynamic drag and an increase of approximately 3 kg in wing mass.

ENGINEERING DECISION

A successful aerodynamic concept that was not the final answer

The design proved that the required downforce could be achieved, but it was not accepted as the final solution because the mass and drag penalties were too large. I used those tradeoffs as the starting point for the next rear-wing iteration.

I also ran the structural stress simulations mandated by the Formula Student rules for the relevant rear-wing components and load cases.
