Published CFD Research - Internal Ducting for Electric Trucks

Co-authored research using ANSYS Fluent to evaluate an internal flow duct as a passive aerodynamic and energy-saving strategy for heavy-duty electric trucks.

RESEARCH QUESTION

Can internal airflow control reduce electric-truck drag?

The study addressed a gap in vehicle-aerodynamics research by examining an internal duct integrated into a simplified electric-truck body rather than relying only on external drag-reduction devices.

METHOD

Baseline vs ducted CFD comparison

The work compared baseline and ducted 2D truck configurations in ANSYS Fluent across 50 to 140 km/h. The analysis evaluated drag coefficient, drag force, propulsion power, energy consumption per kilometer, pressure behavior, velocity fields, and wake development.

CFD VISUALIZATION

Pressure contour comparison

Pressure contour visualization from the ANSYS CFD analysis used to compare the baseline and internally ducted electric-truck configurations.

RESULTS

Lower drag translated into lower energy demand

The ducted configuration achieved up to a 7.5% reduction in drag force and 7.53% lower energy consumption per kilometer. At 140 km/h, the aerodynamic improvement corresponded to an estimated 8.2% extension in driving range for the modeled 400 kWh vehicle.
