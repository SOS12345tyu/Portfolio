ROLE & INTEGRATION

Coordinating the complete mechanical package

I coordinated the body assembly and component positioning across Rolling Chassis, Aerodynamics, Powertrain, and Low Voltage so the complete concept could be packaged as one vehicle rather than as isolated subsystems.

Because I designed the steering wheel and dashboard, I worked directly between the Rolling Chassis and Low Voltage departments and also supported the steering subsystem with its geometry and mechanical integration.

I also worked very closely with the Cost & Manufacturing team because this year's competition work centered heavily on the body and frame, which overlapped directly with my responsibility for the body and aerodynamic package.

Direct design ownership
  • Body panelling
  • Nose cone
  • Front wing
  • Steering wheel
  • Dashboard
  • Support for rear wing and undertray development

ERGO RIG

Validating driver packaging before final chassis decisions

I coordinated with the Rolling Chassis team to produce a preliminary chassis based on their most recent chassis iteration so we could verify that the major cockpit and high-voltage components would physically fit before the final packaging decisions were locked in.

To make the rig representative of the CAD package, I created full-scale MDF mock-ups of the TSAC, steering wheel, pedal box, and dashboard using laser-cut parts made directly to the CAD dimensions.

We then had potential drivers sit in the rig so we could evaluate seating position, driver inclination, reach, clearance, and packaging margins. The results helped us determine how inclined the seating position needed to be and, from that, where the TSAC and firewall could realistically be positioned.

This gave the Rolling Chassis team a physical packaging check before committing to the final chassis geometry and helped reduce the risk of late-stage interference between the driver, controls, TSAC, dashboard, and firewall.

SIMULATION & THEORY

Simulation ownership from model selection to interpretation

I ran the aerodynamic simulation campaign using SolidWorks, ANSYS, Creo, and SimScale. My responsibility went beyond running the software. I researched and selected the appropriate models, defined the analysis approach, carried out verification and validation, compared results between tools, and interpreted the final aerodynamic behavior for the team.

I also ran the structural stress simulations required by the Formula Student rules for the relevant aerodynamic components, using the mandated load cases to check that the designs were structurally acceptable before they were treated as competition-ready concepts.

ENGINEERING DOCUMENTATION

GD&T across 3 vehicle systems

I produced GD&T and manufacturing drawings across 3 major vehicle systems: Aerodynamics, Steering, and Suspension. This helped translate the CAD models into parts that could be dimensioned, checked, manufactured, and assembled consistently.

COST & MANUFACTURING

1st place in Cost & Manufacturing

Our team placed 1st in the Cost & Manufacturing event. I worked closely with the Cost & Manufacturing team because the body and frame were a major focus this year, so many of the decisions they presented were directly connected to the components, materials, manufacturing routes, and assembly strategies I was developing.

The 2026 Real World Task asked the team to produce a plan to reduce total Bill of Material cost spend by 10% without sacrificing the project goals. For our work, I contributed from the aero-kit and body perspective by looking at how the aerodynamic package could be made more cost-efficient while preserving its performance targets.

Real World Task focus: 10% BOM cost reduction without sacrificing project goals, with the brief specifically encouraging benchmarking, make-or-buy studies, material and scrap optimization, and manufacturing-process optimization.

MANUFACTURING STRATEGY

Building an in-house carbon-fiber process

I led the manufacturing strategy for the aerodynamic package. I investigated alternatives to conventional vacuum bagging, explored prepreg carbon-fiber concepts, created an in-house manufacturing plan, and started physical manufacturing trials to verify whether the planned methods could achieve the required mass, finish, and structural targets.

Concept Class clarification: the 2026 FSUK entry was Concept Class, so a complete physical aero kit was not manufactured for competition. The manufacturing work focused on process development and testing so future aero packages can be produced using validated methods.

RESULT

2nd in Design, 1st in Cost & Manufacturing

The 2026 concept placed 2nd in the Design event. The team also placed 1st in the Cost & Manufacturing event, and the aerodynamic package received the full available design points in its evaluation. Across the wider aero-development process, the concept approximately doubled downforce while reducing drag and contributed to an estimated endurance lap-time improvement of almost one minute.

The wider vehicle-development work also reduced mass by approximately 10 kg, while my work with the Rolling Chassis Head reduced chassis mass by approximately 7 kg.
