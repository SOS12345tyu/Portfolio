FORMULA STUDENT · SYSTEM LEAD

2026 Formula Student Concept Class Vehicle

Vehicle integration, aero development, simulation leadership, manufacturing strategy, GD&T, and cross-department coordination for the team's 2026 FSUK Concept Class entry.

ROLE & INTEGRATION

Coordinating the complete mechanical package

I coordinated the body assembly and component positioning across Rolling Chassis, Aerodynamics, Powertrain, and Low Voltage so the complete concept could be packaged as one vehicle rather than as isolated subsystems.

Because I designed the steering wheel and dashboard, I worked directly between the Rolling Chassis and Low Voltage departments and also supported the steering subsystem with its geometry and mechanical integration.

Direct design ownership
  • Body panelling
  • Nose cone
  • Front wing
  • Steering wheel
  • Dashboard
  • Support for rear wing and undertray development

SIMULATION & THEORY

Simulation ownership from model selection to interpretation

I ran the aerodynamic simulation campaign using SolidWorks, ANSYS, Creo, and SimScale. My responsibility went beyond running the software. I researched and selected the appropriate models, defined the analysis approach, carried out verification and validation, compared results between tools, and interpreted the final aerodynamic behavior for the team.

I also ran the structural stress simulations required by the Formula Student rules for the relevant aerodynamic components, using the mandated load cases to check that the designs were structurally acceptable before they were treated as competition-ready concepts.

ENGINEERING DOCUMENTATION

GD&T across 3 vehicle systems

I produced GD&T and manufacturing drawings across 3 major vehicle systems: Aerodynamics, Steering, and Suspension. This helped translate the CAD models into parts that could be dimensioned, checked, manufactured, and assembled consistently.

MANUFACTURING STRATEGY

Building an in-house carbon-fiber process

I led the manufacturing strategy for the aerodynamic package. I investigated alternatives to conventional vacuum bagging, explored prepreg carbon-fiber concepts, created an in-house manufacturing plan, and started physical manufacturing trials to verify whether the planned methods could achieve the required mass, finish, and structural targets.

Concept Class clarification: the 2026 FSUK entry was Concept Class, so a complete physical aero kit was not manufactured for competition. The manufacturing work focused on process development and testing so future aero packages can be produced using validated methods.

RESULT

2nd place in Design and full aero design points

The 2026 concept placed 2nd in the Design event, and the aerodynamic package received the full available design points in its evaluation. Across the wider aero-development process, the concept approximately doubled downforce while reducing drag and contributed to an estimated endurance lap-time improvement of almost one minute.

The wider vehicle-development work also reduced mass by approximately 10 kg, while my work with the Rolling Chassis Head reduced chassis mass by approximately 7 kg.
